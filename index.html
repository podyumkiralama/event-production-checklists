Load-in coordination

Event Crew Access and Work-Zone Matrix

A venue can be open while the correct work area is still unavailable. An access matrix connects each crew call to a route, zone, permit and handover state so teams do not arrive before they can work safely and productively.

Separate arrival from work permission

Record four different moments: vehicle arrival, unloading permission, crew access and work-zone release. They are not interchangeable. A truck may be checked in but unable to reach the loading bay; a crew may enter the venue while the stage area remains under another contractor's control.

Build the matrix by dependency

List the activities that must finish before another crew can begin. Rigging points may need release before lighting motors are loaded; stage legs and decks may need locking before LED ground support is positioned; power distribution may need inspection before audio and video racks are energised.

Use role names rather than individual phone numbers in the public working copy. Keep contact details in the controlled production directory and reference the role responsible for each decision.

Plan congestion and recovery

Mark shared lifts, narrow corridors, security desks and waste routes as constrained resources. Give each a booking or priority rule. Add a holding location for vehicles and a safe staging area for cases so one delay does not block every later delivery.

Change rule: if an access route, work zone or call time changes, update the matrix owner and every dependent crew—not only the team that requested the change.

Minimum access record

  • supplier or department, crew size and vehicle type;
  • arrival, unload, access and work-start windows;
  • gate, bay, lift and final work-zone route;
  • clearance limits and equipment handling method;
  • permit, induction and PPE requirements;
  • zone owner, release condition and fallback area;
  • actual handover time and unresolved exception.
